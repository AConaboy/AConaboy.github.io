PhD Thesis, doctor rerum naturalium

Timing performance studies of the hadronic calorimeter of the Scattering and Neutrino Detector at the LHC and reconstruction of neutrino inelastic scattering events

Dr. Andrew Picot Conaboy
Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin
Doctoral thesis, 2026 Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Heiko Lacker

My doctoral research was carried out as part of the SND@LHC experiment at CERN. I was involved in the construction and testing of the detector in late 2021 as well as the installation of the experiment in early 2022.

Abstract

The SND@LHC experiment was approved in 2021 and subsequently underwent a test-beam campaign before installation in the TI18 service tunnel 480 m away from the ATLAS interaction point. Its purpose is to exploit the intense neutrino flux in the far-forward region of this interaction point. Along with FASERν, SND@LHC constitutes the first experimental effort to study all three flavours of neutrino at LHC energies. These neutrinos offer unique physics insights, including the ability to probe the low-x gluon parton density function and measure the neutrino production cross-sections at LHC energies. This thesis focuses on the upstream section of the muon system (US) of SND@LHC. Presented first are commissioning studies to correct the unwanted time-walk effect; align the timing response of the US read-out channels to one another and other electronic subsystems of the detector; the implementation of a timing model of the US system in the simulation framework of the experiment. Building upon this com- missioning, algorithms for producing spatial measurements using the aligned timing of the US are constructed and validated using experimental data and the timing model in simulation. The spatial measurements are then incorporated in machine learning methods to present the first physics study utilising the US muon system and provide a platform for further analyses using the methods and results of this thesis work.
